| Annualised run-rate of a municipal contract signed in month 10 | $300,000 | Challenge (50.0%) | $150,000 |
| Change in policy capitalising fleet repairs | $85,000 | Reject | $0 |
| Route density savings available to a regional acquirer *(undocumented)* | $220,000 | Reject | $0 |
| **Total** | **$1,135,000** | | **$585,000** |

Where we differ from the seller:

- **Personal expenses run through the company** — reject. Personal spend run through the company is a real add-back only if it can be itemised. Credited in full against invoices; credited at zero otherwise, because an unitemised "personal expenses" line is as likely to be operating cost as it is to be the owner’s boat.
- **Annualised run-rate of a municipal contract signed in month 10** — challenge. Annualising a contract with only a few months of history prices in performance nobody has observed yet. Credited at half with a signed contract and delivered months behind it, a quarter without — and revisited in diligence rather than argued at screening.
- **Change in policy capitalising fleet repairs** — reject. A change in accounting policy moves where a cost appears; it does not change the cash. Credited at zero.
- **Route density savings available to a regional acquirer** — reject. Synergies belong in the buyer’s model, not the seller’s EBITDA. Crediting them pays the seller a multiple, in cash at close, for work the buyer has to do afterwards.

Adjustments that move against the seller:

- **Yard leased from an owner-affiliated LLC below market** — -$125,000. Related-party terms are restated to market in both directions. The buyer inherits the market rate, not the family rate, so this flows through in full — including when it reduces EBITDA.

## Investment view *(mock — no model was called)*

This is an add-back argument, not a valuation argument: the seller's ask of $21.2m is 7.1x of the EBITDA a buyer can actually underwrite, against a 5.0x–7.0x range. Of the $1.1m of claimed adjustments, $585k survives review — the rest is either unevidenced or belongs in a buyer's own model. At the midpoint the paper LBO returns 2.30x / 18.1% with no multiple expansion, which means the return is real but entirely dependent on holding 62.0% recurring revenue through the hold. The fact most likely to change this view is customer concentration — 21.0% largest (Harbour Point REIT) · 55.0% top five — which would move the deal either to a straight pursue or to a structured bid depending on what the contracts say.
